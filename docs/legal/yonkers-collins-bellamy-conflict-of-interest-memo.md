# MEMORANDUM OF LAW

**RE:** Conflicts of interest and incompatibility of position arising from the Yonkers City Council President's concurrent employment as in-house counsel to the Municipal Housing Authority for the City of Yonkers, and from her MHACY supervisor's service as Chair of the Yonkers Zoning Board of Appeals

**STATUS:** Advocacy work product prepared at the request of a party seeking to challenge the arrangement. It states the case *against* the dual roles. It is not a neutral adjudication, not a finding of wrongdoing, and not a legal opinion on which any person should rely without independent counsel. The officials discussed are entitled to a presumption of good faith and to notice and an opportunity to respond before any body acts. Sections XI and XII identify what still must be verified from primary sources before this memorandum is filed anywhere.

---

## I. Questions Presented

1. Whether the President of the Yonkers City Council may lawfully and ethically hold, simultaneously, (a) that elective municipal office and (b) salaried employment as in-house counsel to the Municipal Housing Authority for the City of Yonkers ("MHACY"), a public corporation whose business recurrently comes before the City Council.

2. Whether that arrangement is rendered materially worse — and independently disqualifying — by the fact that the Council President's employer and direct supervisor at MHACY, President and CEO Wilson Kimball, concurrently serves as **Chair of the Yonkers Zoning Board of Appeals** ("ZBA"), a body whose members are appointed by the Mayor subject to City Council confirmation and whose subject matter overlaps the Council's zoning jurisdiction.

3. What relief is available, and to whom the matter should be referred.

---

## II. Short Answer

**Yes as to the conflict; the arrangement should not continue.**

The strongest case does not rest on the simplest theory. General Municipal Law ("GML") § 801 — the core contract-interest prohibition — is *not* the best vehicle, because MHACY is a separate public corporation and the employment contract is with MHACY rather than with the City. Any argument built primarily on § 801 will likely fail, and building on it concedes the field when it does. That concession is made here openly, at the outset, because the case does not need it.

The case that does hold rests on four independent grounds, each sufficient on its own:

1. **Common-law incompatibility of office.** Under *People ex rel. Ryan v. Green*, 58 N.Y. 295 (1874), and *O'Malley v. Macejka*, 44 N.Y.2d 530 (1978), two public positions are incompatible where one is subordinate to, or subject to the review, audit, or oversight of, the other — because holding both frustrates the design that one act as a check on the other. The Council legislates on, appropriates to, and votes on matters concerning MHACY. The Council President is a salaried subordinate within MHACY. She therefore sits on both sides of the check. This is the classic configuration the doctrine exists to forbid, and it does not depend on proving a single bad vote.

2. **GML § 805-a(1)(c).** A municipal officer may not receive compensation for services rendered in relation to any matter before a municipal agency *over which she has jurisdiction or to which she has the power to appoint* any member or officer. The Council President is compensated by MHACY. MHACY's business is transacted before City agencies — including the ZBA, the Planning Board, and the Council itself. The Council confirms mayoral appointments to the ZBA. The statutory elements line up.

3. **The Kimball overlay — structural capture in both directions.** The Council President's paycheck and performance evaluation run through a supervisor who holds a City board seat that the Council President's own body confirms and can decline to reconfirm. Simultaneously, that supervisor presides over the board that rules on variances affecting MHACY and its development partners. Each official holds leverage over the other's position. Neither the Council's oversight of the ZBA nor the ZBA's independence from MHACY survives that arrangement intact.

4. **Appearance of impropriety.** New York courts annul land-use determinations on the appearance of a conflict without proof of actual corruption. *Matter of Tuxedo Conservation & Taxpayers Ass'n v. Town Bd. of Town of Tuxedo*, 69 A.D.2d 320 (2d Dep't 1979). The exposure here is not only to the officials; it is to the *validity of the City's land-use approvals*, which is the City's problem and the taxpayers'.

**Recusal does not cure it.** The defect is structural and continuous — it inheres in the employment relationship itself, not in any particular vote. Recusal addresses discrete votes; it cannot un-employ the officeholder, and it cannot restore the Council's institutional independence in the periods between votes. Where the conflict is inherent rather than episodic, the remedy under New York law is resignation from one of the two positions, not abstention within one of them.

### B. A correction to the framing of the request

The request as posed assumed that Collins-Bellamy *herself* chairs the zoning board. **The public record does not support that, and this memorandum does not assert it.** The ZBA chair is Wilson Kimball — Collins-Bellamy's employer and supervisor at MHACY.

This correction strengthens rather than weakens the case, and it must be stated accurately for two reasons. First, an advocacy filing containing a demonstrably false factual premise about a named official will be dismissed on that basis alone, and will discredit the accurate allegations traveling with it. Second, the actual configuration is the more serious one: a single official holding two seats can at least recuse from one. Here the conflict runs *between two people bound by an employment relationship*, which no recusal by either one dissolves — Kimball does not stop being her boss when Collins-Bellamy leaves the room, and Collins-Bellamy does not stop controlling his confirmation when Kimball gavels a meeting to order.

If she does, a fifth ground arises — the chair of the Council's zoning committee drawing a salary from an entity whose land-use applications that committee reviews. See § XI, Item 4.

## IV. Governing Legal Framework

### A. GML Article 18 (§§ 800–809)

Article 18 is the State's baseline conflicts regime for municipal officers and employees. Relevant provisions:

- **§ 800** — definitions, including "municipal officer or employee" and "interest."
- **§ 801** — prohibits an officer from having an interest in a contract with **the municipality of which he or she is an officer or employee**, where the officer has the power or duty to negotiate, prepare, authorize, or approve the contract or payments under it, to audit bills or claims under it, or to appoint an officer with those powers.
- **§ 802** — exceptions.
- **§ 803** — affirmative duty to disclose an interest in writing, on the official record.
- **§ 804** — contracts made in violation are void.
- **§ 805** — knowing and intentional violation is a **misdemeanor**.
- **§ 805-a** — certain conduct prohibited outright, discussed below.
- **§ 806** — every city must adopt a code of ethics, which may be *more* restrictive than Article 18.
- **§ 808** — authorizes municipal boards of ethics; Yonkers has one.
- **§ 809** — disclosure of officer interests in zoning and planning applications; knowing and intentional violation is a **misdemeanor**.

### B. GML § 805-a(1)(c) — the operative prohibition

No municipal officer or employee shall

> receive, or enter into any agreement, express or implied, for compensation for services to be rendered in relation to any matter before any municipal agency of which he is an officer, member or employee **or of any municipal agency over which he has jurisdiction or to which he has the power to appoint** any member, officer or employee.

Three features make this the sharpest instrument available:

1. It reaches **compensation**, not contracts. It therefore survives the objection that MHACY is a separate corporate entity. The question is not who signs the paycheck; it is whether the officer is *paid for services rendered in relation to matters before* the covered agencies.
2. Its reach extends beyond agencies the officer sits on, to agencies over which the officer has **jurisdiction** or **appointment power**. Confirmation authority over mayoral nominees is appointment power in substance.
3. It has been construed to cover matters that *must be* reviewed or brought before the agency, not only matters formally pending — so the prohibition attaches before an application is docketed.

### C. GML § 809 — mandatory disclosure in land-use applications

Every application for a variance, amendment, change of zoning, plat approval, license, or permit under a municipality's zoning and planning regulations must state the name, residence, and nature and extent of the interest of any officer or employee of the municipality in the applicant. Knowing and intentional violation is a misdemeanor. This provision supplies both a compliance obligation and a discovery roadmap: every MHACY-affiliated application before the ZBA or Planning Board is a document that should have disclosed municipal-officer interests, and either did or did not.

### D. Common-law incompatibility of office

Independent of statute, New York recognizes that two public positions may not be held by one person where:

- one is **subordinate** to the other; or
- one is **subject to the audit, review, or oversight** of the other; or
- there is an **inherent inconsistency** between the duties of the two.

*People ex rel. Ryan v. Green*, 58 N.Y. 295 (1874); *O'Malley v. Macejka*, 44 N.Y.2d 530 (1978) (incompatibility exists where there is a "built-in right of the holder of one position to interfere with that of the other," such that "if both posts were held by the same person, the design that one act as a check on the other would be frustrated"). The doctrine applies to positions of **employment** as well as to offices proper, and its consequence is severe: accepting the second position may operate as an automatic vacatur of the first.

Critically, the doctrine is **prophylactic**. It does not require proof that the officeholder actually abused either position. The structural possibility of interference is the violation.

### E. Yonkers Code of Ethics and Charter

Yonkers has adopted a Code of Ethics (Charter § C1A-23 establishes the Ethics Board), and a municipal code of ethics may impose restrictions **more stringent** than Article 18. GML § 806. The Yonkers Board of Ethics is empowered to investigate complaints, hold hearings, render advisory opinions, recommend discipline, and administer disclosure statements. The full text of the Yonkers provisions must be pulled and applied before filing; see § XI, Item 1.

### F. Appearance of impropriety in land use

New York courts annul municipal land-use determinations tainted by the *appearance* of a conflict, without requiring proof of actual corruption or a quid pro quo. In *Matter of Tuxedo Conservation & Taxpayers Ass'n v. Town Bd. of Town of Tuxedo*, 69 A.D.2d 320 (2d Dep't 1979), the Second Department invalidated an approval where the decisive vote was cast by a member whose firm stood to receive business from an affiliate of the applicant, holding that although "the anathema of the letter of the law may not apply," "the spirit of the law was definitely violated."

This is the Second Department, which governs Westchester County. It is controlling authority here, not persuasive authority.

## V. Argument, Point I — Council President and MHACY Counsel Are Incompatible Positions

### A. The Council sits in judgment on MHACY; the Council President sits inside MHACY

The Council President's public duty is to legislate on, appropriate to, and oversee entities that transact business with the City, MHACY among them. Her private duty as in-house counsel is to advance MHACY's institutional interests. These are not merely different duties; on any contested MHACY matter they are **opposed** duties, and she is obligated to both simultaneously.

That is the *Ryan/O'Malley* fact pattern. The Council is designed to act as a check on entities seeking City action. When the presiding officer of that check draws a salary from the entity being checked, "the design that one act as a check on the other" is frustrated. 44 N.Y.2d at 535.

Note what this argument does **not** require. It does not require identifying a single vote she cast wrongly. It does not require proving she favored MHACY. Incompatibility is assessed from the structure of the two positions, not from the officeholder's conduct within them.

### B. GML § 805-a(1)(c) is satisfied on the face of the arrangement

Element by element:

| Element | Application |
|---|---|
| Municipal officer or employee | Council President is a municipal officer. |
| Receives compensation | Salaried by MHACY. |
| For services to be rendered | In-house counsel services. |
| In relation to any matter before a municipal agency over which she has jurisdiction or to which she has appointment power | MHACY's business — zoning relief, site approvals, PILOTs, land dispositions, cooperation agreements, bond authorizations — is transacted before the ZBA, the Planning Board, the IDA, and the Council itself. The Council has jurisdiction over the last, and confirmation power over ZBA appointments. |

The natural rejoinder is that her legal work for MHACY is internal (personnel, contracts, tenant matters) and does not touch City agencies. That rejoinder is testable, and the burden of producing the evidence sits with her. As general counsel to a housing authority engaged in redevelopment, the presumption runs the other way. Section XII sets out the document demands that would resolve it.

### C. Why § 801 is not the lead argument — stated plainly

MHACY is a public corporation organized under the Public Housing Law, separate from the City. Her employment contract is with MHACY. Section 801 by its terms reaches interests in contracts with "the municipality of which he is an officer or employee." A tribunal is likely to hold that § 801 is not directly triggered.

That concession is deliberate, and it is the reason the argument is framed on §§ 805-a and 809 and on the common law instead. Any complaint that leads with § 801 invites a dismissal that will be reported as vindication on the merits — which it would not be.

**Two § 801 theories are nonetheless preserved.** First, if the City and MHACY are parties to cooperation agreements, PILOT agreements, or intermunicipal contracts on which the Council votes, and if her MHACY compensation is funded in identifiable part by City payments under those instruments, an indirect-interest theory becomes available. Second, GML § 806 permits the Yonkers Code of Ethics to sweep more broadly than § 801, and a local provision reaching employment by entities doing business with the City would apply directly. Both depend on documents not yet obtained.

### D. The appearance problem is a City problem, not only hers

Every Council vote touching MHACY, and every land-use approval in the chain, is exposed to *Tuxedo*-style challenge. An objector need not prove corruption; the appearance suffices. The practical consequence is that the arrangement puts a cloud on the City's own approvals — a risk borne by Yonkers taxpayers and by every applicant whose approval sits downstream of a tainted vote.

## VI. Argument, Point II — The Kimball Overlay Is Independently Disqualifying

This is the heart of the matter, and the point the HUD letter conspicuously does not reach.

### A. Reciprocal leverage: each official holds power over the other's position

- **Kimball → Collins-Bellamy.** As MHACY's President and CEO, Kimball controls her employment: assignments, evaluation, compensation, continued tenure. A subordinate's independence from her employer is not a matter of willpower; it is a matter of structure.
- **Collins-Bellamy → Kimball.** ZBA members are nominated by the Mayor and **confirmed by the City Council**, over which she presides. Kimball's ZBA seat — reported as expiring November 26, 2025 — exists at the sufferance of a body led by his own employee.

Each is positioned to reward or punish the other in the other's domain. Under *O'Malley*, that is precisely the "built-in right of the holder of one position to interfere with that of the other."

### B. The Council's oversight of the ZBA is compromised

The Council's confirmation power over ZBA appointments is the principal democratic check on a board that grants variances from the zoning code the Council itself enacted. When the presiding officer of the confirming body is a salaried subordinate of the nominee, that check does not function. A vote to confirm is a vote about her own supervisor; a vote to reject is a vote against her employer's chief executive.

### C. The ZBA's independence from MHACY is compromised in the opposite direction

Kimball presides over the body that rules on variances. MHACY is a developer of housing in Yonkers, with development partners who appear before City land-use boards. Kimball is also the former Commissioner of Planning and Development, which deepens rather than mitigates the entanglement. Every ZBA matter touching MHACY or its partners raises the question whether the chair is presiding over his own institution's application.

### D. Section 809 disclosure and the documentary trail

GML § 809 requires every variance and zoning application to disclose the interest of any municipal officer or employee in the applicant. Kimball is a municipal officer (ZBA chair). Collins-Bellamy is a municipal officer (Council President). Both are MHACY personnel. **Any MHACY or MHACY-affiliate application before the ZBA or Planning Board was required to disclose those interests.** Whether the required disclosures were made is a discrete, documentary, yes-or-no question — and knowing and intentional failure is a misdemeanor under § 805 and § 809.

This is the single most productive line of inquiry available, because it does not depend on characterizing anyone's motives. Either the disclosure appears on the application or it does not.

### E. Recusal is not an adequate remedy

Three reasons:

1. **The conflict is continuous, not episodic.** She is employed by MHACY on every day of her term, not only on days MHACY appears on the agenda. Legislative influence is exercised through agenda control, committee referral, negotiation, and the decision *not* to bring a matter forward — none of which recusal from a recorded vote touches. The Council presidency is disproportionately an agenda-setting office, which makes this objection stronger, not weaker, than it would be for a rank-and-file member.
2. **Serial recusal is itself a disqualification.** Where an officer must recuse so often that constituents are effectively unrepresented on a category of business, the answer under New York practice is that the officer should not hold both positions.
3. **Recusal cannot reach the reciprocal-leverage problem at all.** Even a perfectly recusing Council President remains Kimball's employee, and Kimball remains subject to her Council's confirmation. Nothing either can abstain from changes that.

## VII. Anticipated Defenses, and the Answers

**1. "HUD has approved it."**
HUD's letter, per the reporting, addresses federal public-housing program requirements. HUD does not administer New York's General Municipal Law, the common-law doctrine of incompatibility, or the Yonkers Code of Ethics, and has no authority to waive them. Federal program eligibility and State conflicts law are separate inquiries; clearance on one is not clearance on the other. And on the record as reported, the letter never addresses the Kimball/ZBA configuration — the ground on which the objection principally rests. The letter should be produced in full and read before this response is finalized.

**2. "MHACY is a separate public corporation, so no City conflict exists."**
Correct as to § 801, and conceded above. Irrelevant to § 805-a(1)(c), which turns on compensation for services related to matters before agencies she has jurisdiction over — not on the identity of the contracting party. Irrelevant to the common-law doctrine, which turns on subordination and inconsistency of duty. And irrelevant to § 809, which turns on the officer's interest in the applicant.

**3. "No New York statute expressly bars a council member from working for a housing authority."**
True, and beside the point. Incompatibility is a **common-law** doctrine precisely because the Legislature did not attempt to enumerate every incompatible pairing. *Ryan* and *O'Malley* supply the test; the absence of a bespoke statute is not a safe harbor. Additionally, GML § 806 authorizes Yonkers to impose stricter local rules, and whether it has done so is an open question of local text.

**4. "She recuses when MHACY matters come up."**
Addressed at § VI.E. Also: this is an assertion of fact that should be tested against the minutes. If the recusal record is incomplete, the defense collapses into an admission.

**5. "The voters knew and elected her anyway."**
Election does not ratify a violation of the General Municipal Law or cure common-law incompatibility; conflicts rules protect the institution and the public, not merely the electorate's information interest. The premise is also weak on its own terms: she prevailed with a 38% plurality in a five-way primary with low turnout, meaning most participating voters preferred someone else — which is an argument about mandate, not about legality, and should be deployed carefully and not as a legal point.

**6. "This is politically motivated."**
Possibly, and irrelevant to whether the elements are met. The response is to keep every allegation tied to a document and to state candidly what has not been verified — which is the posture this memorandum adopts.

## VIII. Relief Sought

1. **Primary relief:** Collins-Bellamy should resign from **one** of the two positions. The structural defect is cured by separation, and by nothing short of it.
2. **Parallel relief:** Kimball should resign the ZBA chairmanship, or the seat, for so long as he supervises a member of the City Council. Relief directed at only one of the two officials leaves the reciprocal-leverage problem intact.
3. **Interim relief pending resolution:** full written disclosure on the record under GML § 803; recusal from all MHACY-related and ZBA-appointment matters; and public identification of every past matter in which recusal was or was not observed.
4. **Institutional relief:** referral for an audit of ZBA and Planning Board files for GML § 809 disclosure compliance on all MHACY-affiliated applications.

## IX. Fora and Referral Strategy

| Forum | Basis | Realistic outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Yonkers Board of Ethics** (Charter § C1A-23; via Law Department, 914-377-6250) | Local code of ethics; advisory opinions; investigation | Advisory opinion or recommendation. Fastest route; note the Board's members are locally appointed, which bears on independence. |
| **NYS Attorney General — Informal Opinion** | AG issues informal opinions on municipal conflicts and incompatibility; the Ledger cites Informal Ops. 1997-22, 1998-38, 2002-7 | Non-binding but highly persuasive; historically the standard route for incompatibility questions. Requests are ordinarily made by the municipal attorney, which is a practical gating issue. |
| **NYS Comptroller** | Long line of Article 18 opinions | Persuasive authority; useful for building the record. |
| **Yonkers Inspector General** | Local investigative authority | Fact development, including the recusal and disclosure record. |
| **Article 78 proceeding** | Challenge to a specific ZBA or Council determination on conflict / appearance grounds, *Tuxedo* | The only forum that produces a binding order — but requires a concrete determination to challenge and strict compliance with the four-month statute of limitations. |
| **Quo warranto — Executive Law § 63-b** | Removal from an office unlawfully held under incompatibility doctrine | The remedy with real teeth, but it is brought by the Attorney General; a private party cannot initiate it directly. |

**Recommended sequence:** FOIL first (§ XII), then Board of Ethics and Inspector General referrals on a documented record, then an AG informal-opinion request. Do not file the Article 78 unless and until a specific tainted determination is identified and is within limitations.

## XIII. Limitations

This memorandum states one side of a contested question. It was prepared from public reporting and from settled principles of New York municipal law; primary-source retrieval was blocked in the drafting environment, and § XI catalogs what remains unverified.
